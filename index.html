About the data

Every fact comes from Wikipedia

What becomes a node. Figures are the members of Wikipedia’s categories People, Athenians and Spartans of the Peloponnesian War, plus figures those articles link to whose Wikipedia-stated lifespans overlap 431–404 BC. Events are the Battles and Naval battles of the Peloponnesian War categories and the war’s campaign navbox, plus linked events dated 446–399 BC. Polities are every combatant named in a battle infobox. Works are plays and histories dated 431–380 BC whose author is a figure in the graph.

What becomes a connection. Commanded and fought come from battle infoboxes (with Wikipedia’s killed-in-action, captured and executed markers). Wrote comes from work infoboxes. Allegiance comes from categories and infoboxes. Linked in prose means one article links to another inside a sentence; that sentence is shown as evidence.

Sides. A figure’s side comes from the Athenians / Spartans of the Peloponnesian War categories, from an infobox allegiance, or from a short description (“Athenian general”). Failing those, it comes from the side the figure commanded on in battle infoboxes. A city-state’s side is the principal belligerent it most often fought alongside in the war’s battle infoboxes. Each card states which rule applied. Alliances shifted during the war, so a side is a summary, not a verdict.

Text. Bios are each article’s first paragraph; stories are a paragraph from a named section; “Did you know” hooks are from Wikipedia’s main-page feature, recorded on article talk pages. Every card links the exact revision it was taken from. Dates are parsed from infoboxes, short descriptions and lead parentheticals; “c.” marks approximate dates.

Images. Only Wikimedia Commons files licensed public domain or CC0 are used, credited on each card. Illustrations labelled “AI illustration” are generated by an image model (named on each card), from a prompt built only from the node’s Wikipedia title and short description. They are decorative, not historical evidence.
